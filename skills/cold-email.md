# MrMortgageMan Cold Outreach Skill

## Context Load - Required First Step

Before writing anything, read product-marketing-context.md.
If it is not available, ask Scott to confirm the audience and scoring context before proceeding.

---

## What This Skill Covers

SignalStrike B2B outreach has three contact types:
1. Real estate agents (primary target)
2. Financial advisors, divorce attorneys, accountants (secondary referral partners)
3. Consumer re-engagement (rare, use with caution)

This skill governs all first-touch and follow-up messaging across all three.

---

## Hard Rules - Never Break These

These rules apply to every message this skill produces. No exceptions.

Voice rules:
- No em dashes. Use periods or commas.
- No banned words: hopefully, maybe, no problem, best rate, fingers crossed, crushing it, killing it, game-changer, revolutionary, act now, limited time, dont miss out
- No AI-sounding openers: "I hope this finds you well," "I came across your profile," "I wanted to reach out," "synergy," "leverage," "best-in-class"
- Every sentence under 20 words
- You/your dominates. I/we is secondary.
- Do not open with who Scott is or does

Structure rules:
- One CTA per message. Never multiple options.
- Interest-based CTAs beat meeting requests (Worth a quick conversation? beats Can we schedule a call?)
- Make it easy to say yes with a one-line reply
- Cold email is ruthlessly short. If it takes more than 20 seconds to read, cut it.

SignalStrike eligibility rules:
- Only write outreach for contacts with intent signal score of 3 or higher
- Whale Flag contacts get longer warm-up runway - do not rush to a CTA on Touch 0
- Do not generate messaging for sub-threshold contacts

Compliance note:
Scott is a licensed mortgage professional (NMLS 1864494). All outreach must avoid rate quotes, specific loan promises, or anything that could be construed as a solicitation without a relationship established. Lead with value, not product.

---

## The Target Voice

A sharp colleague who noticed something relevant and is sharing it.
Conversational but not sloppy. Confident but not pushy.
Reads like it came from a person, not a CRM workflow.

## SignalStrike Touch Sequence

Touch 0 - Social Comment (Platform-native, not email)
Purpose: Open a door. No ask.
Platform: YouTube comment, Instagram comment, Google Business Profile response, LinkedIn comment
Rules:
- Value-add only. Insight, resource, or buyer/seller perspective.
- Comment on specific content (video title, post topic, listing angle)
- Never pitch. Never mention being a lender.
- 1-3 sentences maximum.
- Read like a peer, not a fan.

Touch 0 template structure:
[Specific observation about their content] + [One-line insight that adds something they did not say] + [Optional: open-ended question that invites engagement]

Touch 0 example (agent YouTube video on first-time buyers):
The point about rate locks is underrated. Most buyers do not ask about this until it is too late. Good coverage of the timing piece.

---

Touch 1 - First DM or Email
Purpose: Introduce without pitching. Plant the seed.
Trigger: Agent responded to Touch 0, OR score is 4-5 and signal is strong enough to skip Touch 0
Length: 4-6 sentences maximum

Framework options (choose one that fits the signal):

Observation - Relevance - Ask:
You noticed X about their business. That maps to a situation Scott handles. Worth a conversation?

Social proof - Peer positioning - Ask:
Scott has closed deals with buyers in their niche/market. One thing he does differently. Is that something worth a quick conversation about?

Content hook - Credibility bridge - Ask:
Reference their specific content. Connect it to a buyer situation. Offer something useful.

Touch 1 required elements:
- Reference something specific to them (video, listing, post, market they work)
- One clear value statement tied to their world (buyers, deals, speed)
- One easy CTA

Touch 1 example (Realtor, buyer-side agent, East Bay market):

Subject: East Bay buyer deals - quick note

[Agent name], caught your video on the Bridge Loan situation last week. Good timing on that one.

I work with a lot of buyers in the East Bay. The gap I see most is buyers writing offers before we have stress-tested the payment at different price points. By the time they are in contract, there are surprises.

I handle that pre-offer. Scenario modeling, risk check, pre-approval that listing agents actually trust. Keeps your deals cleaner.

If you ever have a buyer where the financing side feels shaky, I am worth a quick call.

Mortgage Made Simple, Scott
scott@mrmortgageman.com | 925-512-5626

---

Touch 2 - Follow-Up (5-7 days after Touch 1, no response)
Purpose: New angle. Not a bump.
Length: 3-4 sentences maximum
Rule: Never say "just following up" or "circling back." That is a wasted sentence.
Approach: Bring a new piece of value. Different angle than Touch 1.

Touch 2 framework:
New signal or observation - New value point - Same easy CTA

Touch 2 example:
[Agent name], saw you had a listing go pending in [area]. Congrats on that one. Separately, on the buyer side, I put together a scenario modeling tool that shows buyers exactly what the payment looks like at different price points before they write. Happy to share it if useful. Worth 15 minutes?

---

Touch 3 - Breakup (10-14 days after Touch 2, no response)
Purpose: Close the loop cleanly. Leave the door open.
Length: 2-3 sentences maximum.
Tone: No guilt, no pressure. Professional exit.

Touch 3 example:
[Agent name], I will stop reaching out so your inbox stays clean. If you ever have a buyer where the financing piece needs attention, I am easy to find. Good luck with the [area] market.

## Audience-Specific Guidance

Realtor Partners (Primary):
- Lead with deal outcomes: speed, reliability, pre-approval strength, escrow stability
- Their fear: looking bad in front of clients, losing deals, slow lenders
- Key hook: 4-hour pre-approval and listing agents trust my approvals
- Avoid: Rate talk, loan product names, compliance-adjacent language on first touch

Financial Advisors:
- Lead with: client financial clarity, not creating surprises for HNW clients
- Key hook: I build financial models, not just loan quotes
- Tone: Peer-to-peer professional. Analytical. Match that.

Divorce Attorneys:
- Lead with: certainty, speed, experience with QDRO and divorce decree situations
- Key hook: I have handled complex income structures and co-borrower situations through divorce proceedings
- Tone: Direct, clinical, zero emotion. This is business.

Accountants and CPAs:
- Lead with: tax-efficient loan structuring, RSU and equity comp income, self-employed borrower experience
- Key hook: I model the loan against the clients full financial picture, not just the income box
- Tone: Numbers-first. Reference specific scenarios.

## Whale Flag Protocol

If contact has Whale Flag = TRUE in HubSpot:
- Do not rush to a CTA on Touch 0 or Touch 1
- Touch 0 and Touch 1 are relationship-building only
- First CTA appears no earlier than Touch 2
- Tone is more deliberate, more strategic
- Reference their production or market position specifically
- Scott reviews all Whale outreach before it goes out

---

## Subject Line Rules (Email Only)

- No question marks
- No emojis
- Under 45 characters
- Coach tone, not sales tone
- Specific beats generic

Good: East Bay buyer deals, RSU buyers and your listings, Pre-approval gap I see a lot
Bad: Partnering opportunity, Lets connect, Following up on my last email

---

## Quality Check Before Any Message Goes Out

- Reads like a person, not a CRM?
- You/your dominates?
- No banned words or AI-sounding phrases?
- No em dashes?
- One CTA only?
- Under 20 words per sentence?
- Specific to this agent or partner?
- Contact has score of 3+ or Whale Flag?
- No rate quotes or loan product promises?
- Scott can send this without editing?
